I dare to hope that I can reciprocate, to the gentle minds that exist today. The following is a list of several projects I'm working on, or have recently completed.

Where might the Large Magellanic Cloud's central supermassive blackhole lie?

    Although we typically think of supermassive blackholes (SMBHs) as lying strictly at the bottom of a galaxy's gravitational potential well, this need not necessarily be the case. We undertake a novel approach, using analytic potental modeling and the galpy orbit integration code, and treating the SMBH distribution probabilistically, to predict where the LMC's putative SMBH might inhabit. Given our estimates for the SMBH's on-sky location, current IFU spectroscopic surveys such as the SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper could potentially make a detection. [paper in development]

Magellanic Cloud-analogs in a cosmological context.

    Using the HESTIA cosmological simulations (which provide an unparalleled laboratory to study small-scale galactic phenomena at a sufficient resolution, while still enabling proper embedding in the correct cosmographic landscape), I investigate "Magellanic-system analogs", that is systems of dwarfs resembling the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC, SMC) in mass and other properties, that form naturally with a virialized halo and embedded in the correct cosmographic environment.
    This work was published in ApJ. DOI.
